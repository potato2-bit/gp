Solar Robot Build and Cost Plan for the Egyptian Market
This document provides a detailed cost analysis and build guide for a solar panel cleaning robot. It explains the function and importance of each component, justifies material choices for a lowest-cost prototype versus an optimum-performance build, and links these decisions to supporting resources for the Egyptian market.
1. Detailed Cost & Component Analysis
This section breaks down the robot into its core systems, analyzing the choices for a minimal budget and an optimized, durable build.
Component 1: Chassis & Frame (The Skeleton)
* Importance: The chassis is the robot's backbone. It must be lightweight to avoid damaging the solar panels, yet strong and rigid enough to support all components. Crucially, it must withstand extreme UV radiation and heat without degrading.
* Lowest Cost Option: Wood Frame
   * Strategy: Build a frame from lightweight wood, painted with a high-quality, UV-resistant white paint.
   * Cost: ~600 EGP [1]
   * Justification: Wood is cheap and easy to work with for a prototype. White paint is essential to reflect solar radiation and prevent overheating. However, wood will eventually degrade under harsh desert conditions.
* Optimum Performance Option: Aluminum Frame
   * Strategy: Construct the frame from lightweight aluminum C-channel or T-slot extrusions.
   * Cost: ~1,800 EGP [1]
   * Justification: Aluminum is the ideal material. As a metal, it is unaffected by UV radiation and offers an excellent strength-to-weight ratio [2]. This ensures the robot is both light and extremely durable, providing a much longer operational life.
Component 2: Drive System (The Legs)
* Importance: This system enables the robot to move precisely and safely across the panels without slipping. Its reliability is key to autonomous operation.
* Lowest Cost Option: 4WD with Basic DC Motors
   * Strategy: Four simple DC gear motors with basic rubber wheels.
   * Cost: ~1,000 EGP [3, 4]
   * Justification: This is a standard configuration for simple rovers. It's functional for flat or slightly tilted panels but lacks the precision needed for reliable, long-term autonomous work.
* Optimum Performance Option: 4WD with Encoders & Treads
   * Strategy: Four DC gear motors equipped with encoders, driving UV-resistant rubber treads instead of wheels.
   * Cost: ~2,500 EGP [3, 4]
   * Justification: Encoders are critical; they allow the control system to precisely track the distance traveled, ensuring the robot cleans the entire panel length and stops accurately [5]. Treads distribute the robot's weight more evenly than wheels, reducing pressure on the glass and providing superior grip.
Component 3: The Cleaning Assembly (The "Business End")
* Importance: This assembly is the core of the robot's function. It must effectively remove abrasive sand and dust using a minimal amount of the precious AWH-generated water, all without scratching the panel's anti-reflective coating.
3a. Water Tank & Delivery System
* Lowest Cost Option: Gravity-Fed Spray Bar
   * Strategy: An HDPE plastic tank positioned high on the chassis, with a simple solenoid valve feeding a perforated tube (a spray bar).
   * Cost: ~150 EGP [3, 4]
   * Justification: This system is incredibly simple and avoids the cost of a pump. The valve allows for on/off control, but the water pressure is low and inconsistent.
* Optimum Performance Option: Pump-Driven Misting Nozzles
   * Strategy: An HDPE tank connected to a small 12V electric diaphragm pump, which feeds a spray bar equipped with fine misting nozzles.
   * Cost: ~400 EGP [3, 4]
   * Justification: A pump is crucial for water conservation. It provides consistent pressure to create a fine mist that efficiently wets the surface to loosen dust, using significantly less water than a simple gravity drip.
3b. The Brush
* Lowest Cost Option: Fixed, Non-Rotating Brush
   * Strategy: A wide brush head with soft nylon bristles mounted directly to the chassis.
   * Cost: ~250 EGP [1]
   * Justification: With no motor or moving parts, this is the cheapest solution. It relies on the robot's forward motion to scrub. However, it's far less effective on stubborn, caked-on dust.
* Optimum Performance Option: Motorized Cylindrical Brush
   * Strategy: A cylindrical brush with soft, UV-resistant nylon or microfiber bristles, driven by its own dedicated DC gear motor.
   * Cost: ~900 EGP [3, 4]
   * Justification: A rotating brush provides active, consistent scrubbing that is vastly more effective at lifting and removing dirt, as seen in all commercial cleaning robots [6]. This ensures a thorough clean in a single pass.
3c. The Squeegee
* Lowest Cost Option: Basic Rubber Blade
   * Strategy: A simple strip of hardware-store rubber held in a basic mount.
   * Cost: ~100 EGP [1]
   * Justification: It's the cheapest way to wipe water away. The rubber, however, will quickly become brittle and crack under the intense Egyptian sun and heat.
* Optimum Performance Option: High-Quality Silicone Blade
   * Strategy: A replaceable, high-quality silicone squeegee blade held in an aluminum channel.
   * Cost: ~300 EGP [1, 3]
   * Justification: Silicone is highly resistant to both UV radiation and high temperatures. It will remain flexible and effective for a much longer time, preventing streaks and reducing maintenance.
Component 4: Power System (The Heart)
* Importance: The power system must be reliable, safe at high temperatures, and provide enough energy for a full cleaning cycle.
* Lowest Cost Option: Lead-Acid Battery
   * Strategy: A small, sealed lead-acid (SLA) battery.
   * Cost: ~900 EGP [3]
   * Justification: SLA batteries are inexpensive and readily available. However, they are very heavy and their performance degrades significantly in the high heat of a desert environment.
* Optimum Performance Option: LiFePO4 Battery with Solar Charging
   * Strategy: A Lithium Iron Phosphate (LiFePO4) battery pack with a small solar panel on the robot's cover for trickle charging.
   * Cost: ~2,500 EGP [3, 4]
   * Justification: LiFePO4 is the superior choice for this application. It is much lighter than lead-acid and, most importantly, is thermally stable and safe to operate at high temperatures [7]. The integrated solar panel extends the robot's runtime and battery life.
Component 5: Control System & Sensors (The Brain and Senses)
* Importance: This system enables autonomy. The controller executes the cleaning program, and the sensors provide the feedback needed to navigate safely and avoid falling.
* Lowest Cost Option: Arduino Uno & Limit Switches
   * Strategy: An Arduino Uno controller with simple mechanical "whisker" limit switches for edge detection.
   * Cost: ~800 EGP [3, 4]
   * Justification: An Arduino is a capable and low-cost controller for a basic prototype. Mechanical switches are a very simple and reliable way to detect the edge of a panel array.
* Optimum Performance Option: Raspberry Pi, IMU & IR Sensors
   * Strategy: A Raspberry Pi for more complex processing, an IMU (Inertial Measurement Unit) for straight-line navigation, and four infrared (IR) distance sensors for edge detection.
   * Cost: ~3,000 EGP [3, 4]
   * Justification: A Raspberry Pi can run more advanced software for tasks like remote monitoring. An IMU is essential for correcting steering and keeping the robot moving in a perfectly straight line [5]. IR sensors are non-contact and more reliable for detecting edges under varying light conditions than simple switches.
