2. Are Commercial Parts Available to Add in SOLIDWORKS?
Yes, absolutely. But they are not "in" SOLIDWORKS by default. You add them from supplier websites.
This is a standard part of professional engineering design, and it's much easier than you think. You almost never model simple, off-the-shelf parts (COTS) like batteries, motors, or screws. You download them.
Here is the professional workflow:
1. Go to a Supplier Website: Don't just go to a store like RAM Electronics. Go to a major component distributor website. The most famous one is McMaster-Carr.
2. Find Your Part: Search for your component, for example, "12V DC Gear Motor."
3. Download the 3D Model: Next to the part number, there is a "CAD" icon or link. You can select "SOLIDWORKS" (or a neutral format like ".STP" or ".IGS") and click "Download."
4. Add to Your Assembly: You now have a perfect, dimensionally-accurate 3D model of that exact motor. You drop this file directly into your SOLIDWORKS assembly and use "Mates" to attach it to your custom-designed frame.
Why is this the correct workflow?
* Accuracy: Your simulation in Simscape will use the exact mass, inertia, and dimensions of the real-world part.
* Speed: You save dozens of hours by not modeling common parts.
* Bill of Materials (BOM): When you finish your design, SOLIDWORKS can automatically generate a shopping list (a BOM) with the exact part numbers from the suppliers you used.
Resource Links:
* McMaster-Carr: The #1 resource for engineers. Their CAD library is legendary.
   * Link: https://www.mcmaster.com/
* GrabCAD Community: A massive free library where engineers and designers share 3D models. If you need a model for a specific brand-name motor or Raspberry Pi, someone has almost certainly modeled it and shared it here.
   * Link: https://grabcad.com/community/library
