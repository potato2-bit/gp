1. Water Usage: The Calculation
To calculate the water required for manual cleaning, the industry standard formula relies on the surface area of the panels and the "flow rate" needed to flush away dust without scratching.
The Formula


$$\text{Total Water} = \text{Number of Panels} \times \text{Area per Panel} (m^2) \times \text{Water Intensity} (L/m^2)$$
* Standard Industrial Panel Size: $\approx 2.2 \text{ to } 2.5 m^2$ (for modern 500W+ bifacial modules).
* Water Intensity (Wet Cleaning): $\approx 2 \text{ to } 4 \text{ Liters per } m^2$.
   * Note: It can be lower ($0.5 L/m^2$) if using high-pressure specialized trucks, but manual crews with hoses use more.
Example Calculation for 1 MW Plant
* Panels: A 1 MW plant has roughly 2,000 to 2,500 panels (assuming 400W-500W panels).
* Area: $2,500 \text{ panels} \times 2.5 m^2 = 6,250 m^2$.
* Water Intensity: Conservative estimate of $3 L/m^2$.


$$\text{Water per Clean} = 6,250 m^2 \times 3 L = \mathbf{18,750 \text{ Liters (approx. 19 Tons)}}$$
Reference for Water Consumption:
* Source: Sustainability Directory & American Polywater Corp Study
* Data: Standard wet cleaning consumes 0.5 to 4 Liters per $m^2$. A 1 MW plant typically requires ~5,000 to 20,000 Liters per cleaning session depending on the method (truck vs. manual).

2. Cleaning Frequency: The "20-Day Rule"
In the MENA (Middle East & North Africa) region, the optimal frequency for wet cleaning is calculated based on the "Soiling Loss Rate."
Soiling Loss Rate in Egypt
* Daily Loss: In desert areas (like Benban or Western Desert), panels lose 0.4% to 0.8% efficiency per day if not cleaned.1

* The Limit: Most operators do not want efficiency to drop below 95% (a 5% loss).
The Frequency Calculation


$$\text{Frequency (Days)} = \frac{\text{Allowed Loss Limit } (\%)}{\text{Daily Soiling Rate } (\%)}$$
   * Scenario: You want to keep losses under 5%. The desert dust accumulation is 0.5% per day.
   * Calculation: $5\% / 0.5\% = 10 \text{ Days}$.
Real-World Application:
Most manual operators in Egypt cannot afford to clean every 10 days, so they push it to 20–30 days, accepting a higher power loss (up to 10-15%) just before the next wash.
Reference for Soiling Rates:
   * Source: Jiang et al. / ResearchGate and NREL Soiling Map
   * Data: A cleaning frequency of 20 days is often cited as the "economic breakeven point" for desert regions to restore 5% power loss.
   * Egypt Specific: A study in Helwan, Egypt showed soiling losses reaching 66% over long periods without cleaning due to cement dust and desert sand.
