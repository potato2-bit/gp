\begin{center}
  {\LARGE\bfseries\color{zc} Solar Sorption Atmospheric Water Harvesting for Egypt}\\[2pt]
  {\normalsize Project Brief \,--\, a salt-free, off-grid water unit for the new reclaimed lands}
\end{center}
\vspace{2pt}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=zclight,colframe=zc,boxrule=0.6pt,arc=2pt,left=6pt,right=6pt,top=3pt,bottom=3pt]
\textbf{Pitch.} A solar-regenerated desiccant unit that pulls drinking-quality, \hl{salt-free}
water from night air for \hl{off-grid communities in Egypt's new reclaimed lands} --- where the
grid is absent, groundwater is deep and brackish, and refrigeration water machines physically
cannot work.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{The problem}
Agriculture is \hl{80--85\%} of Egypt's water; the new desert-reclamation settlements run on
\hl{fossil groundwater (to 1{,}500\,m), brackish (300--800+ ppm), and depleting}. Their people
need \emph{drinking} water --- today only trucked/bottled water (\hl{5--7 EGP/L}) or desalinating
brackish wells. The incumbent air--water technology, vapour-compression \emph{condensation}
(Watergen, GENAQ, Hydrologic), \hl{stops producing below $\sim$30--35\% RH} --- exactly the arid
interior's night condition. \textbf{That market is unserved.}

\section{Solution \& the right method}
Solar-thermal-regenerated \emph{sorption}: night air adsorbs onto a desiccant; daytime sun
regenerates it; vapour condenses to liquid. Three methods benchmarked on hourly NASA-POWER
climate at \hl{six Egyptian sites}, in EGP:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Method} & \textbf{Works arid?} & \textbf{Water (1 m$^3$ sorbent)} & \textbf{LCOW} \\
\midrule
PV condensation & \textcolor{red}{No} (fails at Benban) & humid-only & 0.5--1.6 EGP/L \\
Silica gel (sorption) & Yes & 20{,}000--33{,}000 L/yr & 0.6--1.0 EGP/L \\
\rowcolor{zclight}\textbf{CaCl$_2$/silica composite} & \textbf{Yes} & \textbf{51{,}000--94{,}000 L/yr} & \textbf{0.40--0.74 EGP/L} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The \hl{CaCl$_2$ composite is best at every site} --- most water \emph{and} lowest cost, and the
only option that holds up in the arid new lands. Its \hl{2.76$\times$ advantage over plain silica
gel is confirmed in our G1-validated CADET model}, not assumed.

\section{Why it is credible --- the engineering is done}
A gated multi-fidelity pipeline:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tier-0} isotherm + isosteric heat (Gate G0 pass; $\sim$43 kJ/mol cross-checked).
  \item \textbf{Tier-1} CADET packed-bed breakthrough vs the LSBU dataset (\textbf{Gate G1 pass}).
  \item \textbf{Tier-2} a \textbf{custom OpenFOAM adsorption solver} written and validated
        (mass-balance V1; Ergun $\Delta p$ V2 to 0.29\%).
  \item \textbf{Surrogate} (water $R^2{=}0.98$) $\rightarrow$ 6-site annual yield, EGP economics, 3D model.
\end{itemize}

\section{Market \& competition}
AWG market \hl{\$4.2--7.9\,bn by 2030, $\sim$9--17\% CAGR}. Sorption peers: SOURCE Global,
Uravu Labs (liquid desiccant --- \emph{dropped silica gel}), Water Harvesting Inc / Atoco
(MOF, low-RH). Condensation peers own the humid/grid segment. In Egypt only \hl{IRSC (Cairo)}
builds AWGs --- the \hl{off-grid arid-land sorption niche is open}.

\section{Economics \& honest verdict}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \hl{$\sim$0.4--0.7 EGP/L} (composite) vs bottled 5--7 EGP/L $\Rightarrow$ \hl{payback $\sim$2--4 yr};
        near-zero \emph{electrical} SEC (0.11--0.17 kWh/kg; regen is solar).
  \item \textbf{Not} irrigation (one feddan needs $\sim$167\,t of sorbent) and \textbf{not} vs
        subsidized Nile tap ($\sim$0.001 EGP/L). It is a \hl{decentralized, salt-free domestic-water}
        product for places the grid and clean groundwater do not reach.
\end{itemize}

\section{The ask / roadmap}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em,topsep=1pt,itemsep=1pt]
  \item Build the prototype on \textbf{cheap silica gel} (full pipeline already validated on it).
  \item Upgrade to a \textbf{CaCl$_2$/silica composite} for the validated $\sim$2.8$\times$ low-RH
        yield; engineer against salt leaching/corrosion. Local \textbf{rice-husk-ash silica} is the
        cost/sustainability path.
  \item \textbf{Pilot} one reclaimed-land settlement; sell the financed \emph{unit}, not the litre.
\end{enumerate}
