\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This document fixes one specific market part for every line of the two
builds defined in the prototype design package: the 4-tile bench condenser
that gets built this week, and the full 69-tile robot that is priced for the
thesis but not purchased. Each line names a manufacturer and part number,
states the governing requirement next to the catalogue specification, gives
the unit price in Egyptian pounds with the price provenance flagged, names
the vendor, and records lead time. Prices marked \liveflag{} were read from
vendor web stores on 12 June 2026; prices marked \indflag{} are indicative
market figures that must be verified at the counter before purchase. The
bench build totals \num{5290}~EGP against the \num{5760}~EGP allowance in
the design report. The full robot prices at \num{221800}~EGP
($\approx$\,\num{4621}~USD), and the energy store alone is \SI{61}{\percent}
of that figure, which confirms the battery-cost warning raised in the REE598
proposal review. All conversions use \num{48}~EGP/USD.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and selection method}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{The two builds}
The design report \cite{nasa_power,kazem2023soiling} sizes a full-scale
robot at 69 TEC1-12706 tiles, \SI{1147}{\Wp} of dedicated PV, a
\SI{698}{\ampere\hour} battery at \SI{12}{\volt}, two geared drive motors of
at least \SI{1.03}{\newton\metre} each, and a \SI{12}{\volt} diaphragm pump
feeding a spray bar, with a daily load of \SI{5.32}{\kilo\watt\hour}. It
also concludes that a 4-tile bench prototype validates the per-tile physics
at a small fraction of the cost, because the condensation model is linear in
tile count. This document therefore sources two distinct part sets:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Build A, bench prototype (buy now).} Four tiles, one cold
        plate, hot-side coolers, instrumentation, and a mains supply. No
        mobility hardware: the bench answers the water-yield question only.
  \item \textbf{Build B, full robot (price only).} Every subsystem of the
        thesis design, priced from the Egyptian market where stock exists
        and from import channels where it does not.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Price provenance and exchange rate}
Vendor stores were queried by script on 12 June 2026. Future Electronics
Egypt (\url{https://store.fut-electronics.com}) returns machine-readable
prices and stock status, and every price taken from it is marked
\liveflag{} with the quoted figure unmodified. RAM Electronics
(\url{https://ram-e-shop.com}) and free-electronic.com refuse scripted
search queries, so their items carry indicative prices marked \indflag{}
from recent market knowledge; these must be confirmed in store before money
changes hands. No indicative figure is presented as a quotation. The
conversion rate is taken as \num{48}~EGP/USD, the same rate used in the
design report; USD figures are rounded to the nearest dollar.

\subsection{Selection criteria}
Each part was selected in three steps. First, the governing requirement was
taken from the design report or the verified results file
(\texttt{output/tec\_robot\_results.json}). Second, candidates were drawn
from Egyptian stock (Future Electronics, RAM Electronics, Amazon.eg, local
solar and marine suppliers) before import channels, because same-day
availability dominates a five-day build schedule. Third, among candidates
that meet the requirement, the choice falls to the one with the best
spec-to-requirement margin per pound, with a documented fallback for every
line that has a single source. Imports are accepted only where no local part
meets the requirement, and each import line carries a customs and lead-time
note.

\subsection{Comparison with the design-report allowance}
The design report carries a prototype allowance of \num{5760}~EGP. The
bench list above lands at \num{5290}~EGP, about \SI{8}{\percent} under, but
the two figures cover different scopes. The report's table included drive
motors, a pump, a brush, a \SI{12}{\volt} battery and a small PV module
(together about \num{2490}~EGP) because it priced a mobile demonstrator.
Build A drops all mobility hardware and instead adds a mains bench supply
and proper instrumentation (about \num{2020}~EGP), since the five-day plan
tests condensation physics indoors, not driving. The water-yield experiment
loses nothing by this trade, and the freed budget absorbs price drift on
the indicative lines. Note also that the TEC tile itself now quotes at
\num{120}~EGP live against the \num{130}~EGP design basis, so the
headline assumption of the whole costing survived contact with the market.

\subsection{Cost structure}
Table~\ref{tab:share} breaks the total down. The energy system is
\SI{77.6}{\percent} of the build, and batteries alone are
\SI{60.9}{\percent}. This is the quantitative confirmation of the REE598
review comment: the earlier proposal carried the battery at an optimistic
figure, and repricing it at real market rates (Section~\ref{sec:battery})
multiplies the energy-store line into the dominant cost of the whole robot.
Any future cost reduction effort should start there, either by shrinking the
night load (fewer tiles, smarter duty cycle) or by accepting a lower
autonomy margin.

\section{Selection rationale by subsystem}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Thermoelectric tiles}
The TEC1-12706 was fixed at design time for availability and
$\Delta T_{max}$ \cite{tec12706ds}, with a costing basis of \num{130}~EGP
per tile. The live check on 12 June 2026 found Future Electronics selling
the part, in stock, at \num{120}~EGP \cite{futelec2026tec}, with a second
listing at \num{250}~EGP for a premium batch. The design basis is therefore
validated with \SI{7.7}{\percent} of headroom. The only open question at
quantity 69 is stock depth, which the web store does not expose; the
procurement plan splits the order across Future Electronics and RAM
Electronics and allows one to two weeks for a restock, since both vendors
import this part continuously.

\subsection{Thermal stack}
Each driven tile rejects up to \SI{90}{\watt} on the hot side (cooling duty
plus electrical input). For the bench, a \SI{95}{\watt}-TDP CPU tower
cooler per tile is the cheapest hardware that meets this with a fan
already fitted, and Cairo's computer-mall used market sells them at about
\num{300}~EGP. For the 69-tile robot, individual CPU coolers would cost
over \num{20000}~EGP and weigh too much; the design instead uses nine
banks of shared extruded fin profile (\SI{120x40}{\milli\metre} section,
about \SI{13}{\centi\metre} of length per tile), cut from \SI{9}{\metre}
of stock profile from a Cairo aluminium supplier, with the 36 fans of the
energy budget \cite{nasa_power} providing forced draft.

\subsection{Drive and brush motors}
\label{sec:motors}
The drive requirement comes from the traction analysis
\cite{siegwart2011mobilerobots}: resistive force \SI{32.0}{\newton} on a
\ang{24} slope, wheel radius \SI{0.03}{\metre}, drivetrain efficiency
\num{0.7}, giving \SI{0.686}{\newton\metre} per motor at the wheel and
\SI{1.03}{\newton\metre} after the 1.5 safety factor. The selected Pololu
\#4745 (37D metal gearmotor, \SI{12}{\volt}, 64~CPR encoder) stalls at
\SI{38}{\kilo\gram\centi\metre} = \SI{3.73}{\newton\metre}
\cite{pololu4745}, which is 3.6 times the requirement; the continuous
operating point sits at \SI{18}{\percent} of stall, where brushed
gearmotors live comfortably, drawing roughly \SI{2}{\ampere} of the
\SI{9.2}{\ampere} stall current. The required wheel speed of
\SI{0.05}{\metre\per\second} corresponds to \SI{15.9}{\rpm}, well inside
the motor's \SI{63}{\rpm} no-load point, so the encoder PI loop commands
about \SI{40}{\percent} PWM in cruise and keeps authority in reserve for
slope transients. The brush motor (Pololu \#4741, 19:1, \SI{208}{\rpm}
no load, \SI{1.37}{\newton\metre} stall \cite{pololu4741}) carries a
1.75$\times$ margin over the \SI{8}{\kilo\gram\centi\metre} roller-brush
estimate. Both are import lines with the customs treatment of
Section~\ref{sec:procurement}; the local fallback appears in
Table~\ref{tab:risk}.

\subsection{Pump and spray}
A cleaning event delivers \SI{3.6}{\litre} over the \SI{230}{\second}
coverage path, a mean demand of \SI{0.94}{\litre\per\minute}, and
flat-fan nozzles want roughly \SI{1.5}{\bar} at the bar. The Seaflo
21-series diaphragm pump (SFDP1-010-035-21) is rated
\SI{3.8}{\litre\per\minute} open flow with a \SI{2.4}{\bar} (35 psi)
pressure switch and draws \SI{4}{\ampere} at load \cite{seaflo2023pump}.
That is a 4.0$\times$ flow margin and a 1.6$\times$ pressure margin, and
the internal pressure switch gives dead-heading protection for free when
the nozzles clog with dust. Seaflo is stocked by Egyptian marine and
agricultural-spray suppliers at \num{1500} to \num{2000}~EGP, so no import
is needed; the \num{320}~EGP brushless pump live-listed at Future
Electronics fails the pressure requirement (\SI{0.3}{\bar} head) and is
rejected despite matching the flow number.

\subsection{Battery}
\label{sec:battery}
The verified energy budget is \SI{5.32}{\kilo\watt\hour\per\day}, of which
\SI{5.30}{\kilo\watt\hour} must come from store during the night harvest.
With a 1.2 design margin, \SI{80}{\percent} depth of discharge and
\SI{95}{\percent} charge efficiency, the design report sizes the bank at
\SI{698}{\ampere\hour} at \SI{12}{\volt}, nominally
\SI{8.4}{\kilo\watt\hour}. Four parallel \SI{12}{\volt}
\SI{200}{\ampere\hour} LiFePO$_4$ packs give \SI{800}{\ampere\hour}
(\SI{9.6}{\kilo\watt\hour} nominal, \SI{15}{\percent} over requirement),
and each cycle delivers \SI{7.7}{\kilo\watt\hour} usable against the
\SI{6.4}{\kilo\watt\hour} margined need.

The REE598 proposal review flagged the earlier battery costing as
optimistic, and the market agrees. A \SI{200}{\ampere\hour} pack
(\SI{2.56}{\kilo\watt\hour}) sells in Cairo solar channels at roughly
\num{32000}~EGP, about \num{667}~USD, which normalises to
\num{469}~USD per \SI{1.8}{\kilo\watt\hour}: inside the realistic 350 to
600~USD band the review demanded, and nowhere near the older optimistic
figure. The four-pack bank therefore costs \num{128000}~EGP and single-handedly
sets the project's cost floor. The onboard robot battery is a separate,
much smaller line: a Dakota Lithium \SI{12}{\volt} \SI{20}{\ampere\hour}
pack \cite{dakota2023battery} (or a local equivalent at roughly half the
price) covers the \SI{13}{\ampere} worst-case mobile draw with a
\SI{20}{\ampere} continuous rating and decouples the robot from the
station bank.

\subsection{PV array}
\label{sec:pv}
The target is \SI{1147}{\Wp} \cite{nasa_power,globalsolaratlas}. Egyptian
distributors stock Tier-1 modules in the 545 to \SI{585}{\Wp} class, so
the practical choices are:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item 2 $\times$ Jinko Tiger Neo 72HL4 \SI{580}{\Wp}
        \cite{jinko2024tigerneo}: \SI{1160}{\Wp}, \SI{1.1}{\percent} over
        target. \textbf{Selected.}
  \item 2 $\times$ LONGi LR5-72HPH-545M \cite{longi2022himo5}:
        \SI{1090}{\Wp}, \SI{5}{\percent} under target; acceptable only if
        the battery margin absorbs the shortfall.
  \item 3 $\times$ LONGi LR5-72HPH-545M: \SI{1635}{\Wp},
        \SI{43}{\percent} over; pays about \num{7000}~EGP for margin the
        store already carries.
\end{itemize}

Two Jinko \SI{580}{\Wp} modules meet the target almost exactly at about
\num{7500}~EGP each (indicative; Cairo wholesale runs near
0.26~USD/\si{\Wp} in small quantity). The LONGi 545 remains the named
drop-in because its datasheet dimensions (\SI{2278x1134x35}{\milli\metre},
\SI{27.5}{\kilo\gram}) already drive the CAD envelope.

\subsection{MPPT charge control}
At a \SI{12}{\volt} bus, \SI{1160}{\Wp} of array implies up to
\SI{86}{\ampere} of charge current, beyond any single affordable
controller. Two EPever Tracer 6415AN units (\SI{60}{\ampere},
\SI{150}{\volt} PV input) \cite{epever2023tracer}, one per module, give
\SI{120}{\ampere} of capacity (1.4$\times$) and keep each PV string
(V$_{oc}\approx\SI{52}{\volt}$) far below the input limit. A future
revision should weigh moving the station to \SI{24}{\volt}, which would
halve the current and allow one controller, but that decision belongs to
the thesis design chapter, not the parts list.

\subsection{Control electronics and sensing}
The architecture pairs a Raspberry Pi 4B \SI{4}{\giga\byte}
\cite{rpi2021brief} running ROS~2 with an ESP32 \cite{esp32ds} doing
hard-real-time PWM, encoder counting and the 1-Wire sensor bus; both are
stocked in Cairo. Sensing follows the bench set: DHT22 for the dew-point
decision \cite{dht22ds}, DS18B20 probes for frost-guarding each cold bank
\cite{ds18b20ds}, INA219 for power telemetry \cite{ina219ds}, and an
MPU-6050 \cite{mpu6050ds} (live at \num{180}~EGP, Future Electronics) for
tilt and odometry fusion. The only line that needed judgement is the
INA219: Future Electronics does not list it, RAM normally does, and the
fallback is a shunt plus the ESP32 ADC, so the line carries no schedule
risk.

\subsection{Order online (Build B, when funded)}
Three lines justify import: the two Pololu gearmotors (no Egyptian vendor
stocks an encoder gearmotor above \SI{1.03}{\newton\metre}) and, optionally,
the Dakota onboard battery, which a local \SI{12}{\volt}
\SI{20}{\ampere\hour} LiFePO$_4$ replaces at half the price if schedule
wins over brand. Panels, MPPT, station batteries, pump and aluminium are
all local supply with one to two week quotes from solar wholesalers.

\subsection{Customs and lead-time note}
Courier imports (Pololu ships DHL/FedEx) attract roughly \SI{5}{\percent}
duty on motors plus \SI{14}{\percent} VAT plus brokerage; experience prices
the landed cost at \num{1.3} to \num{1.4} times the catalogue price plus
freight, which is how the \num{4350}~EGP unit figure for the \#4745
(catalogue 54.95~USD) was built. Allow two to four weeks door to door.
AliExpress is cheaper per unit but routes through Egypt Post customs with
three to six week queues and occasional holds, so it is acceptable only for
non-critical spares. Nothing in Build A depends on any import.

\section{Conclusion}
% =============================================================================

The bench prototype is fully sourceable in Cairo in one day for
\num{5290}~EGP, inside the design report's \num{5760}~EGP allowance, with
every schedule-critical part confirmed in stock by a live vendor query on
12 June 2026. The full robot prices at \num{221800}~EGP
($\approx$\num{4621}~USD at \num{48}~EGP/USD), and the number is dominated
not by the robot itself but by its energy store: batteries are
\SI{61}{\percent} of the total once priced at verified market rates, the
correction the REE598 review asked for. Every line in both tables names a
specific part, a vendor, a margin against its governing requirement and a
fallback, so purchasing can begin without further engineering input.
