Even if you had an unlimited water source right next to the solar park, cleaning with water would essentially be the "premium" option—technically better for the panels, but operationally excessive.
If water availability were not an issue, the ideal strategy would almost certainly shift to a Hybrid Model rather than purely wet or purely dry.
Here is the breakdown of why water is superior in quality, but why robots (dry cleaning) would likely still do the heavy lifting.
1. Why Water IS Better (The Pros)
If you ignore the cost and logistics of water, wet cleaning is technically superior to dry cleaning for three major reasons:
* Removal of "Cemented" Soiling:
   * In the desert, morning dew mixes with dust to create a thin layer of mud that bakes onto the panel (cementation).1

   * Dry robots cannot remove this.2 They often smear it or leave a residue.

   * Water dissolves it. Wet cleaning restores panels to nearly 100% efficiency, whereas dry cleaning typically plateaus at 95-98%.
   * The "Sandpaper" Effect:
   * Dragging a dry brush across a dusty panel is effectively like rubbing it with very fine sandpaper.3 Over 20+ years, this damages the Anti-Reflective Coating (ARC) on the glass.4

   * Water acts as a lubricant, flushing away the abrasive sand particles before the brush touches the glass, significantly extending the lifespan of the panels.5

      * Cooling Bonus:
      * Solar panels lose efficiency when they get hot.6 Water cleaning physically cools the panels, which can provide a temporary 10-15% power boost immediately after washing.

2. Why You Would Still Use Robots (The Cons of Water)
Even with a water source nearby, relying only on wet cleaning is problematic:
         * Thermal Shock: You cannot spray cold water on hot desert panels during the day, or the glass will crack.7 This forces you to clean only at night or early morning, drastically limiting your cleaning window.

         * Water Quality (The Hidden Cost): You cannot just use "water." It must be Demineralized Water.
            * Groundwater in Egyptian deserts (like the Nubian Sandstone Aquifer) is often salty or mineral-heavy.
            * If you spray this water on panels, it will evaporate and leave white mineral spots (scaling) that block sunlight worse than the dust did.8

            * The Cost: You would need to build and run a massive Reverse Osmosis (RO) desalination plant just to clean the panels.
3. The "Hybrid" Solution (The True Winner)
If water were available, the most efficient operators would not switch entirely to water. They would adopt a Hybrid Strategy, which is currently considered the global "Gold Standard" for desert solar:
               1. Daily: Dry Robots (Clean 95% of the dust).
               * Why? It's virtually free once installed and keeps production high every single day.
               2. Monthly/Quarterly: Wet Manual/Truck Cleaning (The "Deep Clean").
               * Why? This uses the available water to wash off the sticky "cemented" mud and bird droppings that the robots missed, resetting the efficiency to 100%.
