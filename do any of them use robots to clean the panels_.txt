Yes, robotic cleaning is actively used in Egypt's solar sector, particularly for the massive industrial stations located in the desert where dust (soiling) is high and water is scarce.1


The most prominent example is the Benban Solar Park, where manual cleaning would be nearly impossible due to the sheer scale of the facility (millions of panels).
1. Benban Solar Park (Industrial Scale)2
* Status: Heavily relies on Robots.
* The Tech: Several developers at Benban use water-free robotic cleaning systems.3 These robots move along the rows of solar panels (often at night) using microfiber cloths and airflow to brush off dust without using a single drop of water.4

* Key Provider: Ecoppia, a world leader in robotic solar cleaning, signed a deal to supply their "Ecoppia T4" robots for large sections of the Benban complex.5 These robots are designed specifically for single-axis trackers (panels that move to follow the sun).6

* Why Robots?
   * Water Scarcity: Benban is in the Western Desert.7 Transporting water for manual washing is expensive and environmentally taxing.

   * Efficiency: Desert dust can reduce energy output by 30-40% if not cleaned.8 Robots clean every night, keeping efficiency at max, whereas manual crews might only clean once every few weeks.

2. Scatec Projects (Industrial Scale)
      * Status: Uses Robots.
      * Details: Scatec (one of the largest developers in Benban) has integrated automated cleaning solutions into their operations. They have worked with research partners (like SINTEF) to optimize robotic cleaning that uses "dry" methods or micro-droplets to avoid scratching the glass in harsh desert sands.
3. Kom Ombo Solar Plant (Industrial Scale)
      * Status: Designed for Automated Cleaning.
      * Details: As a newer plant (operational recently) located just 20km from Benban, it was designed with bifacial panels and automated cleaning protocols to handle the same harsh desert environment, following the successful model established at Benban.
4. Local Innovation (Small/Medium Scale)
      * Status: Emerging.
      * Details: There is a growing local industry for this. Egyptian engineers and startups (such as Winsola and university-incubated projects) have developed locally manufactured solar cleaning robots.9

         * Target: These are often marketed towards the Medium-scale stations (factories, poultry farms, remote oases) that might not afford the massive contracts with global firms like Ecoppia.
